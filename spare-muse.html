MUSE HOSTING / SPARE

Redundancy is a plan, not a spare login.

The brief proposes hosting on a spare Muse, with the owner willing to tolerate its failure. A robust design still needs to define what fails, how it is detected, and how the site comes back.

Questions before relying on a spare

  • Is the spare independently powered, connected, patched, and monitored?
  • Are files replicated safely and regularly, with versioning and an offline or separate backup?
  • Who owns access, incident response, key rotation, and recovery?
  • How will DNS or routing fail over, and how long can propagation take?
  • Has recovery actually been tested from a clean environment?

Failure-friendly design

Keep source in Git, make exports portable, version configuration, and document a manual static fallback. Avoid a single machine as the only copy of either the site or its credentials. Set realistic recovery time and recovery point objectives before promising availability.

“No love lost if anything fails” is a useful risk posture only when important data is backed up and replaceable.
